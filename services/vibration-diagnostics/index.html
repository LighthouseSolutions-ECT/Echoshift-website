Evidence before intervention

Vibration analysis tied to the maintenance question

Useful vibration analysis starts with the right measurement point, a known operating condition and the mechanics of the asset. Frequency content only becomes meaningful when it is tied back to the machine.

EchoShift combines site context with time-domain, frequency-domain and comparison evidence to narrow the problem and rank the mechanisms the measurements actually support.

Diagnostic scope depends on the machine

Speed, load, drive arrangement, access, construction and neighbouring equipment all affect what can be concluded. The visit is scoped around the actual asset rather than applying one generic test to everything.

When to use it

Problems vibration can help investigate

Compare likely mechanical causes with vibration from the structure, surrounding equipment and operating conditions.

01

Imbalance-like behaviour

Strong rotational vibration may indicate an unbalance mechanism, but location, phase/context and machine construction still matter.

02

Misalignment or coupling concern

Harmonic patterns and directional measurements can help determine whether coupling or alignment deserves inspection.

03

Looseness or structural response

Broad harmonics, impacts or location-dependent response can indicate a structural or mechanical transmission problem.

04

Bearing-related vibration

Higher-frequency and envelope evidence can be examined where the measurement path supports it.

05

Gear or driven-train behaviour

Gear-mesh and rotational relationships can be investigated on accessible geared drives and connected machinery.

06

Unexplained machine-to-machine difference

Comparable assets can be measured under similar conditions to identify meaningful differences worth following up.

On-site process

A structured vibration investigation

Start with the machine, its operating condition and the reported change.

01

Understand the asset

Record machine type, speed/load information, drive arrangement, symptom history and recent maintenance.

02

Choose measurement points

Use rigid locations close to bearing and load paths, with orientation and mounting chosen for the question.

03

Analyse the vibration patterns

Examine overall levels, spectra, harmonics, impacts, comparisons and bearing-focused evidence where relevant.

04

State what is supported

Separate likely mechanisms from possibilities and specify the next inspection or measurement needed if uncertainty remains.

Diagnostic output

Turn vibration data into a maintenance decision

A useful vibration diagnostic connects observed features to a maintenance decision.

01

What was measured, how the machine was operating and the relevant drive/mechanical arrangement.

02

03

Questions

Vibration diagnostic FAQs

Practical questions about vibration measurements and diagnostic scope.

Do you just compare vibration to a single limit?

No. Overall vibration can be useful, but diagnosis also depends on frequency content, machine speed, location, direction, operating condition and the mechanics of the asset.

Can you diagnose every fault from vibration alone?

No. Some problems need temperature, speed, electrical, lubrication, inspection or process evidence. A good diagnostic should identify when vibration is insufficient rather than force a label.

Can this be a one-off visit?

Yes. A diagnostic snapshot can be used as a focused one-off investigation. Continuous monitoring is a separate option when trend or intermittent behaviour makes longer observation useful.
