The diagnostic route

Five steps from enquiry to action

The scope can be small — one problem motor — or include multiple assets, but the underlying process stays the same.

01

Tell us what changed

Machine type, site postcode, symptoms, recent work, speed/load information and how many assets are involved.

02

Scope the visit

We identify useful measurement positions, access needs and whether a snapshot, deeper vibration analysis or bearing-focused work best fits the question.

03

Measure on site

Vibration is captured from rigid machine locations while the operating condition and measurement position are recorded.

04

Analyse the evidence

Overall levels, spectra, harmonics, location-to-location behaviour and bearing-focused features are interpreted together.

05

Report the next action

Receive the findings, supporting evidence and a recommended next step: inspection, correction, re-measurement, maintenance planning or monitoring.

On the machine

Good diagnostics start with a good measurement path

For snapshot work, the sensor is mounted to a suitable rigid point close to the bearing or mechanical load path. On compatible ferromagnetic surfaces, a properly engineered magnetic base allows fast temporary measurements without drilling or adhesive bonding.

Where higher repeatability is needed, an existing threaded point, engineered adapter, bonded pad or stud mount can be used. The mounting method is selected for the measurement objective rather than treated as an afterthought.

Usually no drilling for a snapshot

Temporary magnetic mounting is a practical first choice on suitable machinery. Permanent stud mounting remains available where the customer wants a dedicated long-term measurement point.

What we look at

Interpret the vibration in machine context

Different patterns matter depending on machine type, speed, load, location and the mechanism under investigation.

01

Running-speed behaviour

Useful for imbalance-like response and for understanding how vibration tracks shaft rotation.

02

Harmonics & sidebands

Can strengthen evidence around alignment, looseness, modulation, gears and other repeating mechanisms.

03

Bearing-focused vibration

Higher-frequency and envelope analysis can expose rolling-element evidence that is weak in overall vibration alone.

04

Location agreement

Comparing bearing housings, frame points and coupled equipment helps separate source from transmission path.

05

Operating condition

Speed, load, process state and recent maintenance can completely change how the same vibration should be interpreted.

06

Change over time

When one visit cannot answer the question, repeat or continuous measurements can reveal trend and intermittency.

Questions

Before the diagnostic visit

Practical answers before a site visit.

Does the machine have to be faulty before you measure it?

No. A baseline or comparative measurement can also be useful, especially when the machine is important or a similar asset is available for comparison.

Can you tell us exactly when a bearing will fail?

Vibration can support condition assessment and maintenance planning, but an exact remaining-life date is not something a single measurement can responsibly guarantee.

What happens if another measurement would help?

The report identifies the next useful step, which may be a repeat measurement under a different operating condition, an additional machine location or a period of monitoring.
