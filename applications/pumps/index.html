Asset-specific vibration analysis

Separate the mechanical fault from the process effect

Pump vibration can change because of the rotating assembly, the support structure or the hydraulic operating point. EchoShift combines vibration measurements with speed, load and process context so a strong signal is not automatically blamed on the nearest bearing.

A focused diagnostic can help decide whether maintenance should concentrate on bearings, alignment, coupling, mounting, rotating balance, process condition or further hydraulic investigation.

Useful when a pump starts behaving differently

A snapshot is particularly useful after a change in noise or vibration, repeated bearing or seal problems, alignment work, overhaul, or a shift in operating duty.

Maintenance triggers

When to book pump vibration analysis

Pump work usually starts with a symptom: rising vibration, bearing noise, a coupling or alignment concern, or behaviour that changes with flow or load. The visit follows that symptom across the motor-pump train.

01

Vibration or noise has increased at the pump or motor

02

Bearings, seals or couplings are failing more often than expected

03

A pump was recently aligned, rebuilt or returned to service

04

The vibration changes strongly with flow, pressure or operating point

What we investigate

Vibration evidence tied to the machine

Motor and pump vibration are compared across bearing positions with speed and process condition recorded, helping separate rotational mechanics from hydraulic or structural effects.

01

Bearing condition

Measure bearing housings for changes in overall, spectral and higher-frequency vibration.

02

Alignment and coupling

Axial, radial and harmonic behaviour can show when coupling or alignment deserves attention.

03

Imbalance and rotating forces

Running-speed response can help identify rotating-force problems when position and phase context support them.

04

Looseness and support

Impacts, harmonics and location differences can reveal weak feet, pipe strain effects or structural looseness.

05

Hydraulic excitation

Broadband or vane-related vibration that changes with duty can point the investigation toward hydraulic behaviour.

06

Resonance and amplification

A modest forcing input can become severe when the pump, base or pipework responds near a structural resonance.

Measurement strategy

Measure the pump train, not one isolated point

Typical measurements cover the motor and pump bearing housings, with coupling-side, base or structural points added when needed. Flow, pressure, valve position or other operating details are valuable when vibration changes with process state.

On-site vibration analysis: measurements are taken while the machine is operating in a representative condition where safe access allows, with the operating condition recorded alongside the readings.

Questions

Pump vibration FAQs

Practical answers to common questions before an on-site diagnostic.

Can vibration analysis diagnose cavitation?

Vibration can provide useful cavitation-like or hydraulic-excitation evidence, especially when it changes with operating point. Process information such as flow, pressure and suction conditions helps confirm the hydraulic cause.

Do you measure the motor as well as the pump?

Yes. The motor, coupling and pump form one rotating train, so comparing both sides often makes the diagnosis much stronger.

Is a snapshot useful after a pump overhaul?

Yes. A post-maintenance snapshot can check the returned machine state and provide a useful reference for later comparison when operating conditions are repeatable.
