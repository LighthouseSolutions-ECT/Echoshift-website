Asset-specific diagnosis

A conveyor is a system, not one measurement point

Long conveyors contain many rotating elements and structural paths. A useful diagnostic starts with the failed or suspicious section and measures the drive and accessible bearings around that problem.

Snapshot work is targeted to the drive and the accessible components around the reported problem. For longer-term monitoring, fixed sensors are placed only where the mechanical path can provide useful repeatable evidence.

Useful when the drive or a specific station is causing concern

Examples include a noisy gearbox, recurring pulley bearing failure, abnormal drive vibration, a suspected roller/bearing issue, or a conveyor section that has changed behaviour.

What we investigate

What conveyor drive vibration can help investigate

Each pattern is interpreted with machine speed, measurement location and operating condition so the finding stays tied to the actual asset.

01

Drive motor condition

Motor bearing, rotational and structural behaviour can be assessed at the drive.

02

Gearbox condition

Mesh, shaft-order and bearing evidence can be investigated on accessible geared drives.

03

Pulley bearing concern

Head, tail or bend pulley bearings can be measured directly when safe access and a rigid measurement point are available.

04

Roller / local bearing issue

Selected rollers or support bearings can be compared when a local problem has already been identified.

05

Structural transmission

Conveyor frames carry vibration over distance, so local measurements are needed to avoid blaming the wrong component.

06

Load-related behaviour

Material loading and conveyor tension can change vibration; operating condition is recorded where it affects interpretation.

Measurement strategy

Measurements focused on the problem section

Measurement points are chosen around the drive motor, gearbox, pulleys and accessible bearings relevant to the complaint. Large conveyor systems are scoped by section so the diagnostic remains specific and useful.

Diagnostic boundary: EchoShift diagnoses accessible rotating components and local sections. It does not treat one measurement as a full-condition assessment of every roller, idler and structure on a long conveyor.

On-site process

What happens during a conveyor drive diagnostic

The measurement plan follows the mechanics of the asset and the maintenance question.

01

Identify the problem section

Confirm where the noise/vibration/change is occurring and what rotating components sit in that section.

02

Measure the drive and local bearings

Capture the motor, gearbox, pulley or roller positions that can plausibly explain the symptom.

03

Trace transmission through the structure

Compare neighbouring points to distinguish a local source from vibration carried along the conveyor frame.

04

Recommend component-level action

Prioritise bearing, gearbox, drive, pulley, alignment or follow-up monitoring work according to the evidence.

Questions

Conveyor drive vibration FAQs

Scope the machine before attendance so the visit is useful.

Can one sensor monitor a whole conveyor?

A conveyor has many rotating components and vibration transmission paths. Useful monitoring needs measurement points around the specific components being assessed.

Can you check pulley bearings?

Yes, where the bearing housing is safely accessible and provides a useful vibration path.

Can a snapshot help with a noisy gearbox on a conveyor?

Yes. The gearbox, motor and nearby structure can be measured together to determine whether the evidence points toward the geared drive or another source.
