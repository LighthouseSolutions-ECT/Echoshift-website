Asset-specific diagnosis

Mixer vibration has a process dimension

A mixer can be mechanically healthy and still vibrate differently as product level, viscosity, loading or buildup changes. Useful diagnostics record the process state alongside the vibration measurements.

EchoShift looks at the accessible drive train — not just the motor — to separate a drive fault from process-induced loading or structural response.

Useful for recurring or batch-dependent problems

A snapshot is particularly useful when a mixer becomes noisy under certain loads, repeatedly damages bearings or couplings, or one unit behaves differently from the rest of the line.

What we investigate

What mixer vibration can help investigate

Each pattern is interpreted with machine speed, measurement location and operating condition so the finding stays tied to the actual asset.

01

Motor / reducer bearings

Bearing-housing measurements across the motor and reducer can show where higher-frequency or overall vibration is concentrated.

02

Coupling or alignment concern

Drive alignment and coupling behaviour can appear through directional and harmonic changes.

03

Shaft / impeller imbalance

Product buildup, damaged mixing elements or rotating imbalance can increase running-speed response.

04

Long-shaft / structural response

Long shafts, support structures and vessels can introduce resonances that need to be separated from a local bearing problem.

05

Process loading

Viscosity, fill level and batch state can materially change vibration and should be logged with the measurement.

06

Recurring bearing failure

Comparing motor, reducer and shaft-support measurements can help identify whether the bearing is the source or the victim of another mechanical problem.

Measurement strategy

Measure the drive and the support path

Typical points include motor bearings, gearbox/reducer bearing regions, shaft-support bearings and rigid structural points where accessible. The batch/process condition is recorded so the measurement has context.

Diagnostic boundary: Vibration can help separate mechanical and process-related behaviour, but it does not measure product quality, viscosity or vessel internals directly. Process data and inspection remain important when those factors drive the symptom.

On-site process

What happens during a mixer or agitator diagnostic

The measurement plan follows the mechanics of the asset and the maintenance question.

01

Capture process and drive context

Record mixer type, batch/process state, speed, gearbox ratio, fill/load and failure history.

02

Measure the accessible train

Take readings across motor, reducer and shaft-support points rather than assuming the motor tells the whole story.

03

Compare mechanical and process effects

Relate running-speed, bearing, mesh and structural behaviour to the actual operating condition.

04

Recommend the next check

Prioritise bearing, coupling, alignment, buildup/process, structural or monitoring actions according to the evidence.

Questions

Mixer & agitator vibration FAQs

Scope the machine before attendance so the visit is useful.

Can you diagnose a mixer while it is running product?

Usually that is the most useful condition, provided access is safe. The process state needs to be recorded because it can materially influence vibration.

Do you only monitor the mixer motor?

No. Where accessible, the reducer/gearbox, coupling path and shaft-support bearings are part of the diagnostic picture.

What if vibration changes between batches?

That can be diagnostically useful. Repeat measurements with batch/load context can show whether the change follows process condition or a developing mechanical issue.
