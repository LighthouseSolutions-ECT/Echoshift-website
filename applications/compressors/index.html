Asset-specific vibration analysis

Compressor type changes what “normal” looks like

Screw, reciprocating and centrifugal compressors do not produce the same vibration pattern. A useful diagnostic starts by identifying the compressor type, drive arrangement and operating condition before comparing the mechanical evidence.

EchoShift can focus on the motor, coupling or belt drive, accessible bearings, casing and support structure to determine where abnormal vibration is concentrated and what maintenance check should follow.

Useful when vibration has changed from the known machine behaviour

A snapshot can support maintenance when a compressor has become rougher, bearings or drive components are suspect, vibration changes with load, or the package needs a mechanical condition check before further intervention.

What we investigate

Vibration evidence tied to the machine

Vibration is interpreted against compressor type, speed, load and measurement location so expected forcing is separated from a meaningful mechanical change.

01

Motor and drive condition

Motor bearings, coupling or belt-drive behaviour can be assessed as part of the compressor train.

02

Bearing condition

03

Running-speed and harmonics

05

Load-sensitive vibration

06

Type-specific excitation

Reciprocating, screw and centrifugal machines each have characteristic forcing patterns that must be interpreted in the correct machine context.

Measurement strategy

Scope the measurement plan to the compressor type

Measurements focus on rigid, accessible motor, drive, bearing, casing and support locations. Compressor type, speed, load and operating state are recorded so the vibration is interpreted against the actual machine behaviour and duty.

On-site vibration analysis: measurements are taken while the machine is operating in a representative condition where safe access allows, with the operating condition recorded alongside the readings.

Questions

Compressor vibration FAQs

Practical answers to common questions before an on-site diagnostic.

Do you diagnose every type of compressor in the same way?

No. Screw, reciprocating and centrifugal compressors have different forcing patterns, so the measurement plan and interpretation are matched to the compressor type.

Can you assess the motor and compressor drive in one visit?

Yes. Comparing the motor, coupling or belt drive and compressor-side vibration is often useful for locating the source of a mechanical change.

Is continuous monitoring available for compressors?

It can be. Critical compressor packages are often strong candidates for trend monitoring when a one-off snapshot cannot capture intermittent or developing behaviour.
