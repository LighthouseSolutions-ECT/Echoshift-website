Electric motor diagnostics

Electric motor vibration diagnostics

Investigate changing motor vibration at the bearing housings and frame, then relate what is measured to speed, load, coupling and the driven machine.

Asset-specific diagnosis

Start with the motor, then follow the mechanical train

Electric motors often sit at the centre of the maintenance question, but the strongest vibration at the motor does not always originate inside the motor. EchoShift measures the motor in context with its base, coupling and driven equipment where useful.

A snapshot can help decide whether the next step is bearing inspection, alignment/coupling checks, structural work, comparison under load, or longer-term monitoring.

Useful for planned and reactive maintenance

Motor diagnostics can be used when vibration has changed, after maintenance, before a suspected bearing replacement, or when two nominally similar motors behave differently.

What we investigate

What motor vibration can help investigate

Each pattern is interpreted with machine speed, measurement location and operating condition so the finding stays tied to the actual asset.

01

Bearing condition

Drive-end and non-drive-end bearing positions can be examined for changes in overall and higher-frequency vibration.

02

Imbalance-like vibration

Strong running-speed response may support an imbalance mechanism when the location and machine context agree.

03

Alignment / coupling concern

Directional and harmonic behaviour can help decide whether coupling or alignment deserves inspection.

04

Mechanical looseness

Harmonics, impacts and strong location-to-location differences can indicate looseness or a poor structural path.

05

Base or frame response

Soft-foot-like or structural effects can change what is measured at the motor and should not be confused automatically with an internal fault.

06

Electromagnetic influence

Some vibration can be influenced by electromagnetic forcing; vibration can flag the possibility, but electrical evidence may be needed to confirm the mechanism.

Measurement strategy

Where EchoShift measures

Typical points include rigid drive-end and non-drive-end bearing-housing or end-shield locations, with additional frame, foot, coupling-side or driven-machine measurements where they help answer the question.

Diagnostic boundary: Vibration can narrow a motor problem, but it does not replace insulation testing, current analysis, lubrication inspection or electrical checks where those are the decisive evidence.

On-site process

What happens during an electric motor diagnostic

The measurement plan follows the mechanics of the asset and the maintenance question.

01

Confirm motor context

Record speed, load, mounting, coupling, driven asset and recent maintenance.

02

Measure the bearing path

Capture repeatable measurements from rigid points close to the bearing/load path.

03

Compare the evidence

Check overall levels, harmonics, bearing-focused evidence and agreement across positions.

04

Recommend the next action

State what is supported and whether inspection, correction, re-measurement or monitoring is the sensible next move.

Questions

Electric motor vibration FAQs

Scope the machine before attendance so the visit is useful.

Can you check a motor without drilling it?

Usually, yes. A rigid magnetic mount is practical for snapshot measurements on suitable ferromagnetic surfaces. Existing threaded points or other engineered mounting methods can be used when higher repeatability is needed.

Do you only look at the motor bearings?

No. The motor is measured as part of the rotating train. Coupling, base and driven-machine measurements may be added when they help separate the source of the vibration.

Can the motor be monitored continuously later?

Yes, where the asset justifies it. A snapshot can establish the maintenance question first, then a longer deployment can track meaningful change over time.
