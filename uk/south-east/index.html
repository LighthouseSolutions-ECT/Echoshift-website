Thames Valley Oxfordshire and Buckinghamshire

The Thames Valley is an established technology and innovation area, with advanced manufacturing activity in Oxfordshire. Enquiries here should identify the industrial or support plant you want assessed, rather than assume that a site's sector defines the machinery scope.

For Slough, Reading, Bracknell, Wokingham, Newbury, Oxfordshire, Buckinghamshire and Milton Keynes, explain whether the asset directly supports production or serves several operations. Include any standby arrangement and the person who can confirm normal duty.

Surrey Hampshire and Sussex

For Surrey, north and central Hampshire and Sussex sites, give a clear plant reference alongside the symptom. “Pump two” may be understood by one shift but refer to a different unit in a contractor's records.

State whether the issue affects one asset, the changeover between units or the whole assembly. If the equipment is under another party's maintenance responsibility, identify who can authorise the proposed investigation and supply the machine information.

Kent and Medway

For Dartford, Gravesend, Medway, Sheerness and the wider Kent industrial area, identify whether the machine runs steadily, intermittently or alongside other units. Enquiries from Maidstone, Ashford, Canterbury, Dover and Thanet follow the same route.

Build a clear asset comparison brief

  • Postcode, asset type, separate unit references and number of assets.
  • Which unit normally runs, which is standby and whether units run together.
  • The symptom and whether it appears during steady operation or changeover.
  • Known duty, speed, load and recent maintenance for the affected unit.
  • A useful running window, access requirements and the authorising contact.
  • The decision you need to make and your preferred callback details.

Questions about parallel and standby assets

Does the starting monitoring price cover a complete pump set?

The reference is per monitored asset, subject to suitability and agreed scope. Explain the assembly and number of units so we can define what would be monitored and quote the full arrangement, including installation and commissioning separately.

Can a standby machine be compared with the duty unit?

Tell us whether both can operate under a useful comparable duty. A standby unit may have a different operating history, so the basis of the comparison must be agreed rather than assumed.

Do similar machines all need the same investigation?

Not automatically. Start with the affected unit and the maintenance question. We can discuss whether comparison assets add useful information and include them explicitly in the scope.
